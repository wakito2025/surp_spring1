    The MSRDP SPRING-Asia Program Year 2
    The SPRING-Asia Program is an international graduate program that aims to strengthen regional development planning capacities through interdisciplinary and intercultural learning. Students spend the first phase (Year 1) of the program at TU Dortmund University in Germany before proceeding to a partner university like UP SURP for the second phase (Year 2) of their academic training.

    During the Phase II of the SPRING-Asia Program at UP SURP, students complete the academic requirements for the degree of Master of Science in Regional Development Planning (MSRDP). The program combines classroom instruction, field-based learning, planning workshops, and research focused on development issues in the Philippines and growing economies.

    The academic requirements are typically completed within two semesters:

    • First Semester (August to December)
      Students take lecture-based courses providing theoretical and methodological foundations:
      • Plan 203 – Land Use Planning (3 units)
      • Plan 210 – Planning Process (3 units)
      • Plan 214 – Planning Analysis and Techniques (3 units)
      • Plan 219 – Special Problems in Urban Planning (3 units)
      • Plan 299 – Research Methods in Planning (3 units)

    • Second Semester (January to June)
      Focuses on fieldwork, applied planning, and independent thesis research:
      • Plan 210.1 – Planning Workshop (5 units)
      • Plan 229 – Special Problems in Regional Planning (3 units)
      • Plan 300 – Master’s Thesis (6 units)

    The Planning Workshop and thesis components require students to engage directly with local communities, institutions, and development issues. Field study activities may involve travel to partner local government units (LGUs) or project sites outside Metro Manila.

    As a state university and the only national university in the country, UP is supported largely by public funds. As such, international students are charged higher academic fees than Filipino students, in recognition of the public funding that supports the University's mandate to provide accessible education to Filipinos. These fees are known as Education Development Fees paid by resident and non-resident international students.

    This difference in academic fees is not meant to suggest that international students are treated as second-class members of the UP community, nor does it reflect any difference in the value that the University places on them. Rather, it recognizes the nature of UP as a national university and its responsibility in ensuring that public funds primarily support the education of Filipino students, while international students contribute a greater share toward the cost of the education and resources they receive from the university.

      Medical Certificate

      Another requirement for admission into the University is the medical certificate validated by the University Health Service (UHS). This certificate is issued upon the completion of the Physical Examination and Health Assessment (PEHA).

      For students who have already arrived in the Philippines, the PEHA may be done physically at the UHS or in any of their preferred facility in the Philippines. PEHA fees when done at the UHS usually only account for specimen containers, while diagnostic tests, dental examination, and verificatory consultations done outside may range upwards of PhP 2,000.

      For students who have yet to arrive in the Philippines by the start of classes, the diagnostic tests and medical consultations may be done at their preferred facility in closest to them—ensuring that all PEHA forms have been filled out and appropriately signed by an attending physician and dentist. The student will then request a provisional medical certificate from the UHS by submitting their PEHA forms and diagnostic test results via email for UHS evaluation. Students taking this route shall bring the original hard copies of the diagnostic test results and PEHA forms to UP SURP.

    Diploma processing in the university requires the following:

      Clearance > Transcript of Records > Certificate of Graduation > Diploma

      1. Application for Graduation
          To be included in the tentative list of candidates for graduation, students must submit an application for graduation to the OCS. Upon submission, the School Records Evaluator will check and assess each student applying for graduation. If the student has finished all their coursework, then they will be included in the tentative list.

      2. University Clearance
          The student must be cleared/free of all accountabilities, delinquencies, and/or deficiencies in order to receive clearance. This includes the fulfilment of outstanding fees, school work, missing requirements, and uncompleted forms.

          If you opted for in-person payment through the Cash Office, validation of your payment becomes real-time, while online payments might take 1-3 banking days before payment is credited. Upon receipt of the proof of payment (for online transactions), students may monitor the status of their clearance through their CRS account. Processing of the clearance may take 5-10 working days upon crediting of payment. After receiving clearance, only then will the student be able to separately apply for the transcript of records, certificate of graduation, and other documents.

          Kindly note that the months of July-October are the peak period for the university, which can only accommodate 150 applicants per day. Regardless, your application will be queued accordingly.

          While these processes are done by the Office of the College Secretary on the students’ behalf, it is strongly recommended that students are knowledgeable of the procedures and requirements. The School can only extend its services insofar as processing your requirements; the School remains subject to university process timelines, guidelines, and requirements. As such, the School does not have the power to expedite or fast-track the processing of documents.

      3. Transcript of Records
          The transcript of records is applied through the UP ATIN portal accessible via UP Mail. Students may choose to apply on their own or have the OCS apply on their behalf, in which case, said students must provide OCS their UP Mail credentials and passwords.

          Kindly note that the University processes Transcript of Records by batch and it may take around 6-9 months to release.

      4. Certificate of Completion
          In lieu of the Transcript of Records, the OCS may issue a Certificate of Completion of Academic Requirements and a Certificate of Completed courses to students while waiting for their Transcript of Records to be released.

          Kindly bear in mind that these certificates are issued only upon request. The release of such certificates may take up to 2 weeks.

      5. Diploma
          Students need not apply for a diploma. They must only be cleared of all accountabilities, deficiencies, and delinquencies before being eligible to receive their diploma. Six to nine months after graduation, the University issues the Diploma to the School. The School then sends it to TU Dortmund for signature.           Keep in mind that while these processes are done by the Office of the College Secretary on the students’ behalf, it is strongly recommended that students are knowledgeable of the process.     Daily Life and Campus Mobility

    Navigating the 490-hectare campus of UP Diliman and connecting to the broader Metro Manila region is an integral part of daily student life. The campus is well-integrated into public transit systems, offering options that range from internal campus jeepneys to regional light rail lines and ride-hailing applications.

    On-Campus Transportation

    While walking under the campus's famous shade trees remains the preferred mode of travel for most students, the university relies heavily on its internal Jeepney Network. Route designations are visually color-coded on the roofs of the vehicles to help passengers quickly identify their destination.

    Campus Jeepney Color Coding & Routes

    Route Roof / Body Indicators Key Service Area & Direction
    Ikot Yellow Roof Loops continuously counterclockwise within the campus academic oval and residential areas.
    Toki Yellow Roof with Red Edges Loops clockwise around the campus academic loop and peripheral colleges.
    Philcoa Pure Green or Green with Yellow Edges Connects the central campus to Philcoa and Commonwealth Avenue.
    Katipunan Red Roof Connects the campus to Katipunan Avenue (LRT-2 station, UP Town Center, Ateneo de Manila).
    SM North Green Roof with White Edges Connects campus directly to SM City North EDSA and TriNoma transit hubs.
    Pantranco / Quezon Ave. Pure Green or Pure Blue Runs between the campus and Quezon Avenue / West Avenue commercial corridors.


    Student Commuter Tip: Always keep small cash denominations (Php 1, Php 5, Php 10, or Php 20 coins) ready when riding jeepneys. Presenting a valid UP Student ID entitles you to a 20% student discount on traditional and modern jeepney fares.

    Modal Breakdown & Transit Options

    1. Traditional & Modern Jeepneys
      • Traditional Jeepneys: Iconic open-air vehicles operating fixed routes. Pay cash directly to the driver or pass your fare down through fellow passengers ("Pakiabot po", “Pakisuyo po”, “Bayad po”).
      • Modern / Electric Jeepneys (e-Jeeps): Air-conditioned, higher-capacity vehicles serving select routes entering and exiting campus. Fares are slightly higher than traditional jeepneys but often has added air-conditioning for improved comfort.

    2. Ride-Hailing Applications (TNVS)
      • Grab, Indrive, & JoyRide Car: Transport Network Vehicle Services (TNVS) operate heavily around Quezon City. These app-based rides offer safe, direct, door-to-door trips to specific buildings or dormitories, which are especially useful when carrying heavy luggage or returning late at night.
      • Motorcycle Taxis: Services like Angkas, JoyRide, and Move It offer rapid solo commutes through Metro Manila traffic via smartphone apps.
      • Lalamove, GrabExpress, Angkas Padala, Move It: App-based courier services let you rapidly send your package/parcel to locations within Metro Manila.

    3. Metered Street Taxis
      • White metered taxis can be flagged down along major perimeter roads (Commonwealth Avenue, Katipunan Avenue, and CP Garcia Avenue).
      • Ensure the driver turns on the flag down meter before departing. Taxis generally do not cruise inside the quiet inner academic roads, so setting a pickup near main roads or using a ride-hailing app is recommended.

    4. Rail Transits
      Commuting to the neighboring cities in the broader national capital region is made easy via nearby light rail stations:
      • Light Rail Transit (LRT) Line 2 - Katipunan Station: Accessible via the Katipunan Jeepney. Connects east-west from Antipolo to Manila (Recto).
      • Metro Rail Transit (MRT) Line 3 - North Avenue / Quezon Avenue Stations: Accessible via SM North or Quezon Ave Jeepneys. Connects north-south along the central EDSA highway corridor (Cubao, Makati, Pasay).
    Daily Life and Campus Mobility

    The campus hosts several food hubs and is located near several retail and shopping malls.

    UP Diliman Shopping Center – Several notable establishments include Robinsons Easymart for groceries, Power Mac for Apple products, Handyman for hardware and general merchandise, SouthStar Drug for pharmaceutical needs, and Food Court for dining out. Budget: Php 200 and up.

    GyudFood – Several food stands and kiosks are available here. Meal prices start at Php 100.

    Area 2 (J.P. Laurel St.) – Every broke UP student have indulged in budget-friendly meals ranging from carinderias with homecooked dishes to quaint cafes. The street is lined with food stands and hole-in-the-wall eateries which have been part of many students’ UP journey.

    Epsilon Chi Center – Houses one of UP’s homegrown favorite silog place, Rodic’s, which is famous for its tapsilog (a portmanteau for tapa (davory marinated beef), sinangang (fried rice), and itlog (egg)). Other establishments here include Nomu (pizzas and coffee) and 7-eleven.

    Maginhawa St. – This mixed-use area is lined with different shops, cafes, and restaurants. Top picks would be Provenciano and Mr. Jeepney for anyone looking for Filipino food, Mugen (Japanese), Yongmart for Korean grocery needs, Anytime Fitness for your body goals, and many more. While you are here, you may also check the adjacent streets: Malingap and Magiting, which also have hidden gems scattered throughout. Maginhawa is also parallel to Kalayaan Avenue which also houses many food and financial establishments.

    Kiosks – You will see plenty of small kiosks around UP, especially those around the Academic Oval. These kiosks sell the typical broke UP student’s favorite street foods such as kwek-kwek, fish ball, kikiam, and instant noodles. It is public knowledge that pancit canton (instant noodles) in UP beats the one you cook at home.

    UP Town Center – On UP land, on the other side of Katipunan Avenue, is UP Town Center. It is the closest mall to the campus that offers basic student needs such as food, groceries, and supplies. Prices here tend to be on the steeper side.
